## Why the current approach is slow

1. `run_repair_campaign` drafts repairs before establishing that repairs are needed. It then verifies, revises failed units and verifies again. Large articles add model-only proposal assembly both before and after revision.
2. `article_payloads` splits the union of rows rather than packing complete citation proofs. It repeats article fields across chunks and can split one citation's supporting instructions, producing uncertainty that later calls must reconcile.
3. Revision verification rebuilds batches for the full proposed article. Its cache context includes a hash of every proposed article field; even a difficulty/alias/prose edit can reopen evidence that has unchanged source support. Changing actual topic/meaning must still reopen dependent checks.
4. Article-field verification often uses the drafter's chosen supporting-ID subset. A missing qualifier in that subset can cause repair churn even when the original owned citation already contains the qualifier. Context outside ownership is a different issue and must never silently become proof.
5. Models lack the configured KB taxonomy vocabulary in repair inputs, and repeatedly leave unsupported ratings or review prose. These should be rejected/flagged before an expensive semantic pass, rather than discovered again at application.
6. Primary review repeats the model's complete proposed article because machine verification demonstrably misses issues. Removing primary review or trusting a second same-model verdict would trade cost for unmeasured risk.
7. A single long campaign holds the KB lock throughout planning, preventing application of already-reviewed ready plans. We have accumulated proposals while official clearance remains unchanged.
8. Repeated chat source dumps, status polling and full documentation builds after small review batches add operator/model overhead. They do not improve claim grounding.
